SH. 08 · SCHOOL PROJECT · CSR · 2025

Rethinking a L'Oréal product for South Africa by 2030

Analysing the CSR approach of a multinational, its South African subsidiary and one of its flagship products, in order to propose a more sustainable and inclusive alternative.

CONTEXT
Corporate social responsibility course · IPSA
CASE STUDY
L'Oréal · South African subsidiary
TEAM
Group of 6 · one lead per dimension
MY ROLE
Environment lead

01 · THE TOPIC

Turning a weakness into a growth driver

The principle of the project: choose a multinational, one of its foreign subsidiaries and a flagship product, then critically analyse its corporate social responsibility approach by comparing its commitments with reality on the ground.

We worked on L'Oréal in South Africa. Our research question: how can the company reconcile its diversity and inclusion commitments with selling skin-lightening care products, a culturally and socially sensitive issue in the country?

The final goal: convince senior management, with concrete recommendations for 2030 that connect the environmental, social and economic pillars.

02 · THE APPROACH

From critical analysis to recommendations

  1. STEP 1

    Critical analysis

    The company's strengths and weaknesses, the UN Sustainable Development Goals, a materiality matrix and the ISO 26000 principles.

  2. STEP 2

    Local mapping

    The South African context and the subsidiary's stakeholders: who is affected, who is excluded, which relationships to build.

  3. STEP 3

    Eco-design

    Analysis of the current product (product A) and design of a more sustainable product B with the same main function.

  4. STEP 4

    2030 recommendations

    Proposals that bring together the five dimensions: diversity, ethics, environment, local and strategy.

03 · MY ROLE

Environment lead: eco-designing the product

I was in charge of the environmental dimension, focused on the product studied: a niacinamide anti-dark-spot serum.

Life cycle

Describing each stage of product A: raw materials, manufacturing, packaging, transport, use and end of life.

Process tree

Identifying the pollution linked to each stage, both upstream and downstream of use.

Constraints

Analysing the societal, regulatory, competitive and natural constraints on the product.

Product B

Keeping the same main function, but with a safer formula suited to every skin type and recycled packaging.

Brezet wheel

Turning product B into concrete levers: local bio-based ingredients, a concentrated formula, local manufacturing, a refillable bottle and collection.

Future generations

Showing which upcoming crises make this change necessary, and which future customers need it.

05 · SKILLS GAINED

What this project taught me

Corporate responsibility

Understanding a large group's CSR approach and its tools: SDGs, materiality matrix, ISO 26000.

Eco-design

Analysing a product's environmental impact over its whole life cycle and proposing concrete improvements.

Critical thinking

Comparing a company's discourse with its practices, and defending a well-argued point of view.

Stakeholders

Mapping the players in a territory and the relationships a company has with them.

Cross-disciplinary teamwork

Bringing the analyses of five different leads together into a single line of argument.

Persuading

Building a presentation designed to convince management to invest in sustainable development.

07 · CONCLUSION

This project taught me to look at a product beyond its function: where its materials come from, what it pollutes, who it is for and what it says about the values of the company that sells it.

An eco-design mindset I also apply to aeronautics, where environmental issues are becoming unavoidable.
