SH. 09 · RESEARCH PROJECT · 4TH YEAR · 2026

Can a new sustainable fuel help decarbonise aviation?

Techno-economic assessment of a sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) produced from multiple feedstocks, written as a scientific paper in English.

CONTEXT
Electricity generation and hydrogen · IPSA
FORMAT
Scientific paper in English
TEAM
Group of 6
TOOL
COMSOL Multiphysics

01 · THE TOPIC

Beyond what the simulation promises

Decarbonising aviation relies heavily on sustainable aviation fuels (SAF), which can be used in today's aircraft without major modifications. But first-generation pathways often depend on a single feedstock, which limits their deployment.

We studied an innovative SAF produced in a hybrid biorefinery from municipal solid waste, Jatropha and livestock manure. Promising on paper, it still only exists at the simulation stage.

Our stance: that of engineers advising an airline's management. Stay objective, and assess whether this fuel is truly viable, technically, economically and from a regulatory standpoint.

02 · THE APPROACH

An assessment from every angle

  1. STEP 1

    State of the art

    Existing production pathways, their feedstocks, maturity and limitations.

  2. STEP 2

    Certification

    The technical qualification route (ASTM standards) and sustainability frameworks (CORSIA, ReFuelEU), including traceability.

  3. STEP 3

    Risk analysis

    Feedstock, economic, technological integration, regulatory and durability risks, summarised in a risk matrix.

  4. STEP 4

    Technical & economic study

    A CFD simulation of an A320's fuel feed, then an analysis of the pathway's economic viability.

03 · THE TECHNICAL STUDY

Does the fuel reach the engine properly?

A SAF can be chemically compatible and still behave differently in the aircraft's pipes. We therefore modelled in COMSOL a section of the line that feeds an A320's engines, including its bends, in turbulent flow.

Four fuels were compared: pure kerosene, two blends and pure SAF. The simulation shows slightly less favourable flow behaviour with the SAF, especially in the bends.

We drew a concrete solution from this: adding intermediate booster pumps, installed during heavy maintenance checks. A simple, low-cost adaptation compared with replacing the fleet.

07 · CONCLUSION

This project immersed me in a major challenge for my industry: decarbonising aviation. I learned to assess an innovation in all its dimensions, from simulation to certification, through risks and economics.

And above all to stay objective: a fuel that looks promising in simulation still has to prove itself in the real world.
