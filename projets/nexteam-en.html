SH. 01 · 4TH-YEAR ENGINEERING INTERNSHIP · JUNE – SEPT. 2026

Can a drone be released from a helicopter in flight?

A study of how a helicopter's rotor downwash affects a drone released in flight: CFD simulation of the rotor and simulation of the drone's trajectory.

COMPANY
NEXTEAM Launaguet Machining · Design office
ROLE
CFD analyst intern
DURATION
3 months
TOOLS
STAR-CCM+ · MATLAB · CATIA · FreeCAD · Femap

01 · THE TOPIC

A drone released into a rotor's downwash

When a drone is released from a helicopter in flight, it does not fall through still air: it crosses the disturbed flow of the main rotor, with its downwash and blade-tip vortices.

The aim of the internship: understand this aerodynamic environment and simulate the drone's behaviour right after release, to determine when it can be safely deployed.

It was an open-ended topic, which I led from the literature review to building a complete simulation chain.

02 · THE APPROACH

From CFD to trajectory

  1. STEP 1

    Literature review

    Rotor aerodynamics, wake structure, store separation: positioning the topic within the existing literature.

  2. STEP 2

    Rotor CFD

    Rotor modelled in STAR-CCM+ as a virtual disk (Virtual Blade Model), a choice backed by the literature over resolved blades.

  3. STEP 3

    6-DOF trajectory

    Development of a MATLAB chain that uses the CFD field to compute the drone's trajectory and attitude.

  4. STEP 4

    Decision support

    Definition of safety criteria to assess, at every moment of the fall, whether the drone can be deployed.

03 · IN PICTURES

Modelling and meshing

04 · SKILLS GAINED

What this internship taught me

Rotor CFD simulation

Choosing a suitable modelling level (virtual disk), building a computational domain, setting boundary conditions and the turbulence model.

Meshing

Setting up a zone-based refinement strategy and prism layers to capture the boundary layer, balancing accuracy against computational cost.

6-DOF flight dynamics

Coding the equations of motion of a rigid body, integrating them over time and representing attitude with quaternions.

Data coupling

Exporting a CFD field, interpolating it in MATLAB and using it to compute aerodynamic loads along a trajectory.

Scientific visualisation

Developing my own post-processing tools: sections, streamlines, 3D views and trajectory animations.

Research & autonomy

Conducting a literature review in English on an open topic, justifying every modelling choice and keeping a critical eye on my own results.

06 · CONCLUSION

This internship allowed me to carry out an applied research topic from start to finish: starting from an open question, relying on the literature to make well-argued modelling choices, then building a complete and reusable simulation chain, from CFD in STAR-CCM+ to visualisation tools in MATLAB.

Above all, I take away a method: justify every assumption, keep a critical eye on my own results and identify what would allow me to go further. An experience that confirmed my interest in numerical simulation applied to aeronautics.
