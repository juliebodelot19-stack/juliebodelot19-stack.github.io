SH. 05 · CAD PROJECT · 4TH YEAR · 2026

A fully parametric fencing épée in CATIA

Designing a single CAD model able to generate every version of an épée: regulation size, right- or left-handed, grip type, electric or non-electric.

CONTEXT
CATIA parametric design module · IPSA
TEAM
Group project of 4
MY CONTRIBUTION
Complex surface parts in Shape Design
TOOL
CATIA V5

01 · THE TOPIC

A made-to-measure weapon, a single file

In fencing, weapon dimensions are strictly regulated by the International Fencing Federation, but every fencer has their own preferences: hand, grip shape, blade type, electric or "dry" fencing.

Rather than drawing each version separately, the goal was to create a single, smart model: you choose your options and the whole épée updates automatically, while staying compliant with the rules.

A real industrial use case: allowing a manufacturer to offer a personalised, made-to-measure weapon.

02 · THE APPROACH

From part to parameter-driven assembly

  1. STEP 1

    Breakdown

    Break the épée down into parts (blade, guard, grips, pommel, electrical contact…) and define the parameters of each.

  2. STEP 2

    Modelling

    Part Design for mechanical parts, Generative Shape Design for complex shapes: multi-section surfaces, thickening.

  3. STEP 3

    Parameterisation

    Knowledgeware formulas, rules and checks in each part, so that it adapts on its own to the chosen parameters.

  4. STEP 4

    Driven assembly

    Everything is controlled from the assembly through external parameters and a configuration table linked to Excel.

04 · SKILLS GAINED

What this project taught me

Surface modelling

Creating smooth, complex shapes in Generative Shape Design: guide profiles, multi-section surfaces, conversion to solids.

Parametric design

Driving geometry with parameters and ratios rather than fixed dimensions, to keep realistic proportions.

Knowledgeware

Writing formulas, activation rules and checks to automate the model and secure the values entered.

Driven assembly

Passing parameters from the assembly down to the parts and managing contacts between parts as the geometry changes.

Teamwork

Standardising our design methods so that parts modelled separately work together.

Time management

Running an ambitious project alongside a busy semester, by focusing on what matters most.

06 · CONCLUSION

This project brought together everything I had learned about CATIA during my studies, and took it further with parametric design: moving from a drawn part to a model that adapts on its own.

Above all, it strengthened my surface modelling skills and taught me how to design a complete, consistent product as a team.
